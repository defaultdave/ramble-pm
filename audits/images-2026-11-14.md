# Experience images audit, 2026-11-14

The owner asked for it on 2026-11-14 (lane `audit-images`). This is an audit only: no code was changed. It reads `main` at `7a26999`. One Explore agent did the code reading, and the PM checked the `sizes` strings and the image config against `main`. Nothing was measured: no photos display locally (see IMG-6).

## How images work today

- **Source.** The `photos` table (`schema/catalog.ts:279-319`) stores `url`, `width`, `height`, `byteSize` and a required `blurDataUrl` (a 10 px JPEG). Ingest re-encodes to JPEG q82 and caps the long edge at 2400 px; uploads are 1600 to 8192 px. Photos live on Vercel Blob. The cover is the photo at position 0, and it is also the gallery lead. Seed covers are 3:2.
- **One component.** Every render goes through `Photo` (`components/Photo/Photo.tsx`): `next/image` with `fill`, `object-fit: cover`, `placeholder="blur"` and quality 75. Its `preload` prop also sets `fetchPriority="high"`. There is no plain `<img>`.
- **Config** (`lib/images.ts`): webp only, `qualities: [75]`, `deviceSizes: [640, 1080, 1440, 1920]`, `imageSizes: [160, 320, 480]`, cached for a year. The list is kept short on purpose because Hobby allows 5,000 transformations a month (ADR 0001).

**Cache sharing between home and detail.** On a phone the home card and the detail lead already ask for the same file (`w=1440` at 3x, `w=1080` at 2x), so the lead is a cache hit after the card has loaded. That happens by accident: the card's `100vw` overstates its 342 px frame. On desktop they ask for different files (1080 and 1920), so the lead downloads from scratch.

## The idea: the small cached image under the large one

**The idea.** Homepage cards and the experience page share one small width. A larger view shows that small image, already cached from the card, while the larger one loads, then swaps to the larger one.

**How it maps onto the code.** `Photo` gets an optional underlay. The large view draws a second `<img>` behind the main one, using exactly the card's `srcset` and `sizes` (`cardPhotoSizes.vertical`). Given the same viewport and DPR, the browser picks the same candidate the card picked, so the underlay is a cache hit and needs no new transformation width. The main image fades in over it once it has loaded. The blur stays as the bottom layer for a cold visit.

**Why it fits.**
- Every site is `fill` plus `object-fit: cover` in a fixed-ratio frame, so the two layers crop the same.
- The cover is the lead, so both layers come from the same source.
- One format and one quality mean one URL per width.
- Files are cached for a year.

**What it buys.**
- On desktop: a sharp 1080w image on the first paint of the detail page after a card click, instead of a blur followed by the 1920w.
- On phones: nothing much, because card and lead already share a file (see above).

**Open questions, for a spike to answer with numbers:**
1. **Cold landing.** With no card visited, the underlay is one more request competing with the lead. It is smaller, so it may paint first and lower LCP, or it may slow the lead. The spike measures both.
2. **LCP accounting.** The lead painting at the same size after the underlay is not a larger candidate, so LCP would probably record the underlay's paint. Chrome ignores low-entropy images, though, and a 1080w image stretched to 1584 px may or may not pass that test. This is an inference that needs checking.
3. **Crop.** The card is 3:2, the mobile lead 4:3 and the desktop lead about 1.8:1. `cover` makes them line up only if both layers share `object-position`.
4. **Scope.** Is it the lead only, or the side photos and the guide cover as well?
5. **Measurement first.** IMG-6 must be fixed, or a preview with Blob photos used, before anything can be shown to work.

**Recommendation:** a spike unit on the lead only, measured on a preview deployment with real photos, with the cold and warm cases on desktop and mobile. IMG-1 and IMG-3 are cheap fixes that can ship on their own. IMG-2 waits for the spike's answer.
